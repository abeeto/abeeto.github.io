# My story since graduating from Canada one year on!
Hello,

It's been a year since I've graduated with a BSc in Computer Science from Canada. And I'll be honest, for a long time, I didn't know what I was doing. While I did reasonably well academically, it did feel like I just slapped on cruise control my entire time as a student, and by the time 5 years had gone by, I reached my destination and I was not prepared.

After gradaution I decided to go visit my parents who are working in the UK and my grandparents who live in India. But against the advice of others, I chose to apply for mywork permit for Canada from outside. People warned me of the long wait times, but I never thought it would take as long as it has (10 months). I wanted a break and also some time to figure out things, and boy, did I get a lot of time.

While at the start it was fun to see my parents and meet some friends, it started to dawn on me that my life and future plans  was being decided by the status of my passport. Once my vistor visa in the UK expired, I was back in India with my grandparents, I often couldn't help but feel incredibly powerless and confused by the world. For months, I was incredibly frustrated and angry at Canada's immigration system, I felt I was owed a job and an opportunity to live and work in Canada. After studying and having so many friends there for the past five years, I felt, naively, that it was my god-given right to get a work permit and a simple, straight-forward path to settle there.
 
You may be wondering why this guy seems so upset that he's in India.. and honestly, valid. To those who don't know me, while I am Indian by ethnicity, and most of my family are in India, I, unfortunately, didn't grow up in India. I grew up in the safe suburban bubble of a country that is Brunei Darussalam (which probably deserves a whole post of its own). So it was challenging to find myself back in India, a place that I had visited only during my summer holidays.

It's not that I have anything against being in India; it's just not what I or anyone in my life had envisioned for me. I had imagined a life in Canada. And the truth is, while I have an Indian passport, I often feel like an outsider in my own country. I didn't know who to blame or be angry towards. Canada's immigration policy or my own life choices that led me here.

And while I still haven't heard back regarding my work permit, these weird emotionally charged months, have been deeply transformative. My time in India, while still sheltered (as I mostly stay with my grandparents) has really changed the way I look at life. I see so many hardworking and talented people here in India, who are all so driven and hungry for the chance of a better life. 

Being here, showed me, how much privelege I have benefited from, and I realise that no one owes me anything. Not Canada's immigration system, not a company to give me a job, and especially not my parents. I've been truly blessed for all the opportunities, experiences and memories I've been given so far. India has inspired me to love the process. Because everything here feels like a work in progress. 

I had to re-evaluate a lot of things in my life and I realise that, at the end of the day, I need to be in charge of my own sense of worth, purpose and happiness. I should not be beholden to systems that are, frankly, out of my hands. 

I have this quiet feeling that if I can just be a little bit more consistent and keep continuing to learn and push myself, everything will sort itself out in due time.


So here's a breakdown of 2025:
- I applied for Google Summer of Code in 2025 (will likely write a post on this)
	- I got rejected, but I am now a full stack engineer intern at that same organisation 
- I continued learning more JavaScript, and I'm finally getting closer to finishing The Odin Project's JavaScript course -- which has been a long time coming
- I've learned a lot from contributing to Open Source Projects; something I would have never imagined myself doing before

So yeah this has been a little personal recap of the past twelve months so far. A message to myself 6 months down the line: I hope you keep going and try to focus on the process. The truth is we are exactly where we need to be. 

And by the off-chance anyone else reads this: - thank you. We all tend to share only the achievements and good things, hardly mentioning the challenges and hardships we face. But I often find that how we deal with these hardships defines who we are. So I dedicate this post to anyone whose finding that things aren't going the way you had hoped for. It's okay, keep going. Sometimes the best journeys are the ones you never planned on taking.
